# Market card

Read Real Audit `references/monetization.md` (Pricing evaluation and Evidence sections) read-only. It has the rules for value, willingness-to-pay and measurement. This file adds only the forward-looking parts for ideas that do not exist yet. Every claim carries a URL and access date, or the label [Varsayım]/unverified.

## Per candidate

1. **Existing products:** who already does this (direct and adjacent), their price and package, and how users describe them in reviews.
   - If nobody does it, say so. Name where you searched. A search that finds nothing is not proof of no competitor.
2. **Precedents:** similar features or products that succeeded or failed, and the stated reason. Precedent suggests. It does not prove.
3. **Customer and willingness to pay:** who pays (user, employer, insurer). Give the signals of willingness to pay: competitor prices, paid tiers in the category, price complaints in reviews.
   - A competitor price is a reference point. It is not the willingness to pay for this product.
4. **Revenue range:** a Fermi estimate. Write each input out (reachable users, conversion, price, period) as low / base / high. Label the result [Varsayım].
   - Do not give a single number. Do not present it as a forecast.
   - If the product has real baseline data (installs, paywall views, conversion), use it and cite it.
5. **Cost and effort:** rough build and running cost, and dependencies. Examples: third parties, approvals, policy such as App Store or health-data rules, checked against current sources.
6. **Cheapest test:** the smallest action that would move the revenue range or kill the idea. State which result counts as a pass.
   - Examples: a fake-door button, a landing page with a price, five customer calls.

## Verdict line

Write one sentence for each candidate: likely to work / uncertain / unlikely. Add the single fact that would change that verdict.
